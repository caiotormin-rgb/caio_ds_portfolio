# The public site

Drawn from actually reading the nine portfolios in the CareerFoundry piece
(Maggie Wolff, Naledi Hollbruegge, James Le, Yan Holtz et al.), not from the
article's summary of them.

## What they all actually do

Every one of them keeps the **index thin**: a card or bullet per project, an
image, and *one sentence*. Not one paragraph — one sentence. Depth is always
one click down, never on the front page.

Yan Holtz — the one praised for design — uses explicit **graduated detail
levels**: headline → 2–3 sentence summary → medium sections → long-form project
page. That is the three-layer rule, independently arrived at by a professional.

## The bar is lower than expected

Most of these portfolios show **no methodology, no outcome numbers, and no
limitations** anywhere. One-line descriptions linking to a GitHub repo is the
norm at the level being praised as exemplary.

**Implication for Caio:** the artifact chain in Track A puts him well above this
bar. So the risk is NOT being too shallow. The risk is **burying the depth where
nobody clicks**. Front page must stay ruthlessly thin, and each card must give
the reader a reason to go deeper — which means a *result*, not a topic.

## Card format — the whole index

Per project: **image + title + one sentence containing the finding's number.**

- Weak (the industry norm): "Analysis of municipal spending data using panel regression."
- Strong: "Municipal health spending stops buying outcomes past R$X per capita —
  here's the reallocation."

The second one gets clicked. The first is a topic; the second is a finding.

## Rules
- Index fits on one screen. If it scrolls a lot, there are too many projects.
- Image before text on every card — all of them do this, and it works.
- Track A and Track B visually distinguished but on the same page. The contrast
  is the point: rigor *and* curiosity.
- 3–5 projects total. The article's read: curation reads as confidence.

## Track B has a model worth studying
The article points at [The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/2020/07/gendered-descriptions/)
as the visual-data-essay form. That's the reference for Track B's shape.

## Naledi's move, worth stealing for Track B
She explicitly ties project selection to what she cares about, and the article
singles that out as her strength. Caio's Track B projects should say plainly why
the question mattered to him. That's not decoration — it's the differentiator
the article actually rewards.
