# Track B — Personal interest projects

History, neuroscience, demographics, macro/social change. These are where Caio's
voice lives. Track A's artifact chain would strangle them.

**Looser form. Same discipline.**

## What stays (non-negotiable — these are the ADHD guardrails, not formality)
- One locked central question. Written down before starting.
- A hard chart budget.
- A `DECISIONS.md`.
- An honest limitations passage, wherever it naturally falls.

## What goes
- The 00–07 numbered artifact chain
- Data dictionaries, model cards, activation one-pagers
- Any obligation to name a "stakeholder" or a "business decision"
- Fixed section order

## The form: an inquiry, not a report

Lightly academic — it can cite, it can wander, it can say "I don't know."
It should NOT pretend a corporate audience exists. A rough shape, not a template:

1. **The question**, and honestly why it nagged at Caio
2. **What he expected to find** — written down before looking. Committing to a
   prior in public is the single strongest credibility move available here,
   and it costs nothing.
3. **What the data actually said**
4. **Where he was wrong** — the most valuable section in the whole portfolio.
   Nobody fakes this well, so it reads as real.
5. **What he still can't explain** — open threads, left open

Cite sources properly. Link the data. Let the prose breathe.

## Length
Longer than Track A is fine — up to ~1,500 words — because the reader here is
choosing to read, not scanning to screen. But the first paragraph still has to
earn the rest.

## What Track B is FOR
Track A proves Caio can do the job. Track B proves he's someone worth talking
to for an hour. Interviewers ask about Track B projects. Hiring managers screen
on Track A ones. Both jobs matter; don't let either format contaminate the other.
